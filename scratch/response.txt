Executive Summary

I would recommend Option 3, but with one important conceptual refinement:

Do not think of KUL/DTU as teaching the model "attention." Think of them as teaching the model "EEG feature extraction."

Your AASD model should remain the task-specific model. KUL/DTU should provide a pretrained physiological representation, not the final decoder.

This separates representation learning from task learning, which is the standard strategy in modern transfer learning.

First Principle: What knowledge actually transfers?

The biggest mistake would be assuming that because all three datasets are "AAD," the entire model should transfer.

It should not.

Different parts of the model learn different things.

EEG
 ↓
Spatial Processing
 ↓
Temporal EEG Features
 ↓
Attention Representation
 ↓
Task Decoder
 ↓
Prediction

Each stage has different transferability.

Component	Transferability	Reason
Low-level temporal filters	High	Auditory cortical responses are shared across tasks.
Mid-level EEG representations	Moderate	Envelope tracking and auditory rhythms generalize reasonably well.
Subject-specific spatial mapping	Low	Electrode placement, skull conductivity, anatomy differ.
Sustained-attention decoder	Low	Different task objective from switching.
Switching detector	None	KUL/DTU contain no switching labels.

This immediately rules out transferring an entire pretrained decoder.

Those are not equivalent.

The frozen decoder has never learned:

Even if preprocessing is perfectly aligned, the decoder objective is mismatched.

Your earlier KUL↔DTU transfer worked because both datasets solved essentially the same task.

Here they do not.

Train the Neural Ridge Hybrid entirely on KUL/DTU.

Training the entire architecture on KUL/DTU does not magically create knowledge that isn't in the labels.

Should the encoder come from MatchNet or Conformer?

This is actually an important design choice.

MatchNet learns

representation

↓

similarity

Conformer learns

representation

↓

reconstruction

Your downstream task is

reconstruction

↓

CUSUM

That makes the Conformer encoder a more natural starting point because its objective is closer to the downstream objective.

The MatchNet encoder may still learn useful EEG representations, but its optimization target (contrastive discrimination) differs more from the reconstruction-based Neural Ridge pipeline.

Rather than assuming one is superior, treat this as an empirical question:

Pretrained Conformer encoder → Personalized Neural Ridge

Pretrained MatchNet encoder → Personalized Neural Ridge

Compare both under the same calibration protocol.

This cleanly separates responsibilities:

Recommendation

I would rank the options as follows:

Rank	Strategy	Assessment
1	Hybrid transfer (Option 3, refined)	Strongest. Uses KUL/DTU to learn transferable EEG representations while reserving AASD for subject adaptation and switch-specific learning.
2	Train the Neural Ridge architecture on KUL/DTU first, but only as an encoder pretraining stage—not expecting it to learn switching.	Potentially useful if the architecture itself is retained, but its outputs should still be adapted on AASD.
3	Directly reuse a frozen KUL or DTU decoder after mechanical channel alignment.	Weakest. Decoder objectives are tied to sustained attention and are unlikely to model switching behavior or personalized reconstruction effectively.

The key scientific principle is that tasks transfer poorly, representations transfer well. Use KUL and DTU to pretrain a universal auditory EEG encoder, but let the AASD-specific Neural Ridge model learn what only AASD contains: personalized spatial mappings, reconstruction behavior, and the temporal dynamics of attention switching. This architecture best matches both the empirical evidence from your experiments and established transfer-learning practice in EEG decoding.
